A .gds holds one skeleton per LOD. Loaded by 0x800f3b10. The skeleton's bones are referenced by animation tracks and bone-attached collision by their name hash.

OffsetSizeTypeDescription
0x0064f32[16]Inverse bind matrix, row-major, for row vectors (v * M); it can carry scale

0x497u8[7]0

Rest Pose (0x30 bytes)

f32[4] rotation quaternion (x, y, z, w), f32[3] scale and a pad, f32[3] translation in centimetres and a pad.

Building the Skinning Palette (0x800cab00)

For each bone, in index order:

  1. Local matrix: the rotation matrix of the quaternion (0x8000bd00, the same as System.Numerics.Matrix4x4.CreateFromQuaternion), each row scaled by the matching scale component, and the translation × 0.00125 (1/800) on the bottom row.
  2. World: local × parent's world. The root's world is its local matrix.
  3. Palette: inverse bind × world, kept as a 3 × 4 matrix.

Both products are affine: element (r, c) is fma(a[r][2], b[2][c], fma(a[r][0], b[0][c], a[r][1] * b[1][c])), and the bottom row adds b's translation last. The last column is never computed.

Skinning on the Wii (0x80149dc0)

For each 32-byte vertex: M = Σ weight[k] × palette[joint[k]] over the four joints, then the position (s16, /16384) and normal (s8, /64) are multiplied by M and written back as s16 and s8 to the instance's skinned output. The weights are not normalized. The draw then applies the mesh scale of 8.0. The PS3, Xbox 360 and 3DS skin on the GPU from the same palettes.

Verified: The Wii palette builder (0x800cab00) was given bone poses sampled from real animations. For 10 characters and 30 random clips and times, all 25,020 palette floats came out bit-identical. The quaternion-to-matrix routine (0x8000bd00) matches System.Numerics to 2e-8.

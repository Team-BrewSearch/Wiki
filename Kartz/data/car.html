A .car is a rigid vehicle or prop model built from the same chunks a track uses, with a tail that says where its wheels sit. The layout below is the two-Kartz shape; the later games move the chunk table field from +0x10 to +0x14.

Layout

Twenty-two of the twenty-seven Kartz models carry one chunk and a 72-byte tail; the rest carry two chunks or a longer middle block. Later games leave gaps between chunks where the Kartz games pack them.

Wheel Block

The last 48 bytes of the file are four wheel positions, each an x, y, z triple in 20.12. The height at +0x08 matches the y of every wheel.

In OmegaStudio

  • The chunk count is 1 to 256. The table field is +0x10, or +0x14 if that fails; the table must start at or after 0x20 and fit the file.
  • Each chunk offset must be at or past the end of the previous chunk, and each chunk needs a non-zero dlSize that is a multiple of 4, at most 4096 texture references, and room in the file. The tail starts where the last chunk ends.
  • The wheel block is read only when at least 48 bytes follow the last chunk. Wheel coordinates are edited as decimals in the Properties pane.
  • Replacing from glTF puts a node or mesh named chunk_<n> into chunk n, and anything unnamed into the first chunk. The writer keeps the header, packs the chunks back to back, copies empty chunks and the tail byte for byte, and shifts every header word that points into the tail. If the geometry comes back unchanged, the original bytes are kept.

All 103 .car files across the five games re-serialise to their original bytes and survive an unedited glTF round trip. All 27 Kartz models come back byte for byte.
